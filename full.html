TL;DR · the plan in five lines

The Islamabad Accords in five lines

  • End the October 7th Firestorm: ceasefire on all fronts, Palestine (Gaza, the West Bank), Lebanon and Yemen included, then negotiate it into a long-term armistice
  • Three concurrent deals: split Hormuz’s lanes between Iranian and Omani waters, blend down Iran’s highly enriched uranium, and agree on the contours of the post-war architecture, in exchange for sanctions relief and unfrozen assets, with a cut for regional reconstruction, prioritizing Gaza and Lebanon
  • The clearing-and-settlement loop: Iran delivers, a regional institution verifies, and relief moves, gate by gate
  • The post-war operating system: five regionally owned institutions for the files that sparked the war, open to new ones: a peace table, a nuclear consortium, a Hormuz commission, an arms control forum, and a reconstruction fund
  • The long game: an Iran that never develops a nuclear weapon, inside a region that settles its own disputes: fifteen years of détente, then full normalization in 20 to 30

in the Consortium

Nuclear

Iran & its allies commit

  • Near-zero enrichment for 10–15 yrs · one protected research cascade below 1%, under Consortium verification
  • Every other facility sealed under continuous Consortium and IAEA watch
  • Zero HEU for the duration
  • The existing stockpile part downblended in place and kept under seal, the rest exported to Consortium custody

  • Recognition of Iran's right to enrich under the NPT, via the Consortium, including on Iranian soil
  • Then a supervised ramp to a 1.5–3.67% ceiling
  • Saudi Arabia and any other domestic enrichment program under the same verification
  • The four nuclear gates of card 1 paid as each closes

FIVE TRADES

X.COM/THEKINGDAVIDJR

⚛️

Nuclear · why this bargain

Why near-zero enrichment

  • Iranian enrichment has been effectively on ice since the 12-day war · a single research cascade keeps Iran’s right to a civilian program while formalizing the status quo
  • Regionalized verification comes first, so every dollar released has the direct stakeholders’ blessing
  • Governing a regional consortium would be an extremely complex endeavor, but it’s still the only entity both sides have agreed to at one time or another
  • Near-zero reflects the post-war balance of power: the program largely destroyed, the government that wants it still intact

Hormuz · why a commission

Why a shared strait

  • Geography is king · the strait runs partly through Iranian territorial waters, and Iran has legitimate sovereign rights and interests in the waterway
  • Iran has proven it can impose real costs on anyone using the strait
  • It has also proven it can’t impose its will on the Gulf · America has partly reopened the strait to crude, at staggering cost, and Gulf producers are shipping some of it again
  • Neither side can keep that up for long · escorts, ship-to-ship transfers and blockades are an expensive stopgap
  • The Commission gives everyone an off-ramp that reflects the post-war balance of power, and a way to restore the strait’s economic value for the region and the world

Arms & Presence · in all honesty

Can the region add rules to the jungle?

  • Proxy warfare and defense spending are likely to increase, not decrease · a classic prisoner’s dilemma: each side arms because it can’t trust the other to stop
  • The question is whether adversarial relationships can be managed
  • Rules to the jungle, to stop another disaster from happening · matched restraint, verified step by step, is the classic way out of a prisoner’s dilemma
  • Lower priority than the five trades
  • Some framework for Iran’s proxy support and ballistic missiles is necessary for full statutory sanctions relief from America

Limit 1 of 7

It needs Trump

  • This runs on one man's personal diplomacy
  • Nobody else has the appetite to own it or the standing to move the region
  • He also has to get the entire region on board: the Arabs, Israel and Iran
  • The architecture is built to outlive him, but nothing gets built without him

LIMITS

X.COM/THEKINGDAVIDJR

⚠️

Limit 2 of 7

Nothing is truly irreversible

  • Irreversibility is an ideal, constrained by politics
  • Every element can be undone by a party willing to pay the cost
  • What the architecture changes is how slow, how visible, and how damaging reversal becomes
  • Finality has chargebacks, and geopolitics files more of them than finance does
  • And finality covers acts, not conditions: compliance is continuous, so a party that stops performing ends the series, and ending the series can undo much of what it was building

Limit 3 of 7

No central enforcement

  • The institutions negotiate standards and produce receipts, not adjudications
  • Enforcement is left to the distributed anarchy of geopolitics

Limit 4 of 7

A weak security guarantee

  • Nobody is trapped in this
  • The largest source of leverage in any deal is the ability to walk away from it

“What if Israel undermines it?”

  • Israel’s permanence is a given, and nothing moves the Middle East more than its existential security interests

  • Undermining it carries an increasingly high cost with the American nation

The obvious objections · 6 of 9

“What about Yemen?”

  • Yemen’s political settlement is out of scope for V1: it needs its own political process
  • It is the likeliest place for the All-Fronts Hudna to break first, because Bab el-Mandeb is a choke point and reopening it is existential for Saudi Arabia

  • The infrastructure for that process is already laid: the Table, the Hudna agreements, and the Hormuz Commission as a template for Bab el-Mandeb, as a sister body or a separate file and working group
  • The Fund’s regional interconnectedness can reach the Yemeni front too, as an extension of the architecture after V1

The obvious objections · 7 of 9

“What about Lebanon?”

  • In scope: the All-Fronts Hudna covers Lebanon explicitly, because the Lebanon file has to be handled, and the Fund’s public trust rebuilds it alongside Gaza
  • The Hudna aligns Hezbollah with Hamas behind the 20-point plan
  • With the Axis behind the same ending, Lebanon stops being a second campaign and becomes the leftover of a war both sides have ended on paper
  • That opens room for movement on demilitarizing southern Lebanon, something realistic to break the deadlock over the stalled Israel–Lebanon framework
  • The goal: properly demilitarize south of the Litani, through the official Israel–Lebanon framework, with the Hudna as the side channel that brings Hezbollah along

The obvious objections · 8 of 9

“What stops someone from spoiling it?”

  • Build it spoiler-proof and the universe builds a better spoiler, so it’s built to resist

  • If it breaks anyway, the deals already done still count toward ending the war, and the pieces stand on their own: the Hormuz Commission and the Consortium can be built later, one at a time
  • Its cost is whatever the others are willing to impose
