Three measures, kept apart (the World Bank/UN damage, loss and needs method): damage is assets destroyed; lost output is growth and revenue against pre-war forecasts; war spending is what governments spent. Adding them double-counts, so there is no grand total. Sources: FDD, 'Evaluating the Economic Damage to Iran from Operation Epic Fury', 23 Apr 2026 ($144B, range $50–300B: ≈$91B physical replacement, ≈$53B hydrocarbon revenue); final Gaza RDNA, 20 Apr 2026 (damage $35.2B, losses $22.7B, needs $71.4B); World Bank Lebanon RDNA, 7 Mar 2025 (damage $6.8B, losses $7.2B) and Lebanon's government, Jul 2026 ($3–4B damage in 2026), World Bank Lebanon Economic Monitor 2026 ($3.57B lost); Globes, 10 Mar 2026 (Israel's direct missile damage, about NIS 3B in 2025); World Bank GCC growth, Jan 2026 +4.4% to Oct 2026 −4.3%, on a GDP of about $2.4T (the ≈$210B is computed here); Bank of Israel Annual Report 2025 (NIS 175B lost output to end-2025; NIS 350B war spending 2023–26), at 3.575 shekels to the dollar, the 2024–25 average; Egypt's presidency (Suez revenue lost, about $9–10B by 2026); Costs of War, Brown, 7 Oct 2025 ($31.35–33.77B to Sep 2025); Hegseth to the Senate, 21 Jul 2026 ($37.5B for the 2026 war). Not counted: the West Bank, Syria, Yemen, Jordan, Iraq, US households' costs (about $100B, Moody's), and the world economy's 2026 loss ($0.6–1.3 trillion, Institute for Economics and Peace).
