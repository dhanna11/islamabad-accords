Resistance, not proof: nothing is spoiler-proof, so the architecture is built to resist. Once it exists it is the region's only system, and wrecking it means choosing nothing over something, in public, against everyone who relies on it (Keohane, After Hegemony: institutions outlive their builders because rebuilding is costly). Gains are turned into physical facts as early as possible (money paid out and spent, uranium downblended, the deconfliction cell staffed), and those outlast any one government. The cost of wrecking it is whatever the others are willing and able to impose; a determined government can still pay it, and in the first year there is little to lose. The institutions most likely to survive a collapse are the Hormuz Commission and the Arms Control Forum, then the Consortium; the Fund survives if its money runs outside the dollar system.
